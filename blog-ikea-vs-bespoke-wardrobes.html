IKEA Fitted Wardrobes vs Bespoke Carpenter: Which Is Best?

← Back to blog

If you're looking for fitted wardrobes, you may be wondering whether to choose an IKEA system such as PAX or have your wardrobes designed and built by a carpenter.

Both options have their advantages. IKEA can offer a practical and more affordable starting point, while bespoke carpentry gives you complete control over the design, size and finish.

There is also a third option: combining IKEA wardrobes with bespoke carpentry to achieve a more built-in, high-end appearance.

At Maltings Carpentry & Renovations, we offer this hybrid approach for suitable projects (alongside flat-pack assembly and fully bespoke fitted furniture), helping homeowners across Suffolk get the look they want while making the most of their budget.

IKEA Fitted Wardrobes: Pros & Cons

IKEA PAX wardrobes are popular because they offer a flexible modular storage system. You can choose different sizes, doors, drawers, shelves and internal fittings to create a wardrobe that suits your needs.

Pros of IKEA wardrobes

  • Lower initial cost: IKEA can be considerably more affordable than fully bespoke furniture.
  • Flexible storage: plenty of options for shelves, drawers, rails and accessories.
  • Easy to plan: IKEA's planning tools make it straightforward to create a layout before buying.
  • Good for standard rooms: if your bedroom has straight walls and suits the available IKEA dimensions, PAX can be a practical solution.
  • Modular: components can potentially be changed or rearranged if your storage needs change.

Cons of IKEA wardrobes

The main limitation is that you're working with standard sizes. Your wall might not perfectly match the dimensions of the wardrobe, leaving gaps at the sides or above, particularly noticeable in older Suffolk properties, like the period homes we work on around Framlingham and Woodbridge, where walls, ceilings and floors aren't always perfectly straight.

IKEA wardrobes can also retain a more recognisable flat-pack appearance, particularly when installed without additional finishing work, and assembly and installation can take time, especially when several units need to be accurately levelled and fitted together.

Bespoke Fitted Wardrobes by a Carpenter

A carpenter can design and build your fitted wardrobes specifically around your room, made to suit the exact width, height and depth available rather than the other way around.

Pros of bespoke wardrobes

  • Made specifically for your room
  • Excellent use of awkward spaces
  • More design and material choices
  • A genuinely built-in appearance
  • Ideal for alcoves and sloping ceilings
  • Greater control over doors, handles and finishes
  • Can complement period and character properties

Bespoke furniture can be particularly useful in homes with chimney breasts, alcoves, uneven walls, sloping ceilings or unusual room layouts. Our eaves wardrobes with a window seat and floor-to-ceiling wardrobes are good examples. A carpenter can also add details such as cornices, plinths, end panels, mouldings and scribed sections to create a high-quality fitted appearance.

Cons of bespoke wardrobes

The main disadvantage is cost (our guide to what a carpenter costs explains why). Because everything is individually designed, made and installed, bespoke wardrobes will generally require a larger investment than buying a modular IKEA system. They can also take longer to complete because there may be a design stage, site measurements, manufacturing and installation.

The Best of Both? IKEA + Bespoke Carpentry

You don't necessarily have to choose between IKEA and completely bespoke furniture. For some customers, we offer a hybrid fitted wardrobe service, using IKEA units as the starting point and then adding bespoke carpentry to create a more built-in finish.

This can be a great option if you like the practicality and storage options offered by IKEA but don't want the finished installation to look like standard flat-pack furniture. Depending on the room, we can install the IKEA units and then add carpentry details such as:

  • Bespoke filler panels
  • Plinths and cornices
  • End panels
  • Decorative mouldings
  • Built-in surrounds
  • Additional shelving or storage
  • Adjustments around alcoves or awkward spaces
  • Bespoke finishing details

The aim is to make the installation feel like purpose-built fitted furniture rather than a collection of flat-pack units. It's a useful middle ground between a standard IKEA installation and having every part of the wardrobe made completely from scratch. The suitability and cost will depend on the room, the IKEA system and how much bespoke work is required.

Which Fitted Wardrobe Option Is Right for You?

Choose IKEA if:

  • You're working with a tighter budget.
  • Your room suits standard dimensions.
  • You're happy with IKEA's available styles.
  • You want a flexible modular storage system.
  • You don't mind a more furniture-like appearance.

Choose bespoke carpentry if:

  • You want a truly built-in finish.
  • Your room has awkward dimensions.
  • You have sloping ceilings or uneven walls.
  • You want complete control over the design.
  • You're renovating a period or character property.
  • You want specific materials, doors or finishes.

Consider the hybrid approach if:

  • You like IKEA PAX but want it to look more bespoke.
  • You want to make better use of the available space.
  • You'd like additional carpentry details and finishing.
  • You want to balance the overall cost with a higher-end appearance.

IKEA Fitted Wardrobe FAQs

More answers on our FAQ page.

Can a carpenter make IKEA PAX wardrobes look built in?

Yes. Additional carpentry can be used to fill gaps, create surrounds and add finishing details around IKEA units, helping them achieve a much more built-in appearance.

Can you install IKEA PAX wardrobes?

Yes. For suitable projects, we can install IKEA wardrobe systems and carry out additional carpentry to create a more bespoke finished result.

Is IKEA cheaper than bespoke fitted wardrobes?

The furniture itself is generally less expensive than fully bespoke furniture. However, the final cost of an IKEA-based installation will depend on assembly, installation and any additional carpentry required.

Can IKEA wardrobes look expensive?

Yes. Careful installation and bespoke finishing details such as panels, mouldings, plinths and cornices can make a significant difference to the final appearance.

Are bespoke fitted wardrobes worth it?

For many homeowners, bespoke wardrobes are worthwhile because they can make better use of the available space and give you much greater control over the design and finish.

Can you build wardrobes around sloping ceilings and alcoves?

Yes. Bespoke carpentry is particularly useful for awkward spaces such as alcoves, sloping ceilings, chimney breasts and uneven walls.

How much do fitted wardrobes cost?

There isn't a standard price because costs vary depending on the size, materials, design, doors, internal storage and installation requirements. An IKEA-based hybrid installation can sometimes provide a middle ground between a standard wardrobe and fully bespoke furniture.

Do you cover my area?

We're based in Snape and cover much of Suffolk, including Aldeburgh, Saxmundham, Woodbridge, Framlingham, Ipswich and surrounding areas. If you're unsure, get in touch with your postcode and project details.
